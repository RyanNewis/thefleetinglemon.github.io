## The Review

There are heavy albums, and then there's *Panopticon*. Released in 2004, ISIS's third full-length is a sprawling, hypnotic exercise in tension, atmosphere and sheer sonic weight. Guitars rise and collapse like enormous concrete structures, while Aaron Turner's vocals emerge from the murk like someone shouting from the bottom of a very deep hole. Yet for all its crushing heaviness, there's something strangely beautiful about this record. The quieter passages aren't simply pauses between riffs; they're essential parts of the journey, allowing the music to breathe before everything comes crashing down again.

## Standout Tracks

*So Did We* opens proceedings with a thunderous statement of intent, establishing the album's balance between punishing riffs and expansive atmosphere. *In Fiction* is arguably the centrepiece, building patiently from delicate, almost meditative beginnings into an overwhelming wall of sound. Meanwhile, *Wills Dissolve* demonstrates the band's remarkable ability to make repetition feel genuinely transformative, gradually shifting familiar musical ideas into something entirely new. These aren't songs designed for casual listening. They demand your attention, and they reward it.

## Final Thoughts

Two decades on, *Panopticon* remains a towering achievement in heavy music. Its influence can be heard across countless post-metal and atmospheric sludge records, but few have managed to capture the same combination of emotional depth, restraint and devastating power. It's an album best experienced from beginning to end, preferably through decent headphones or at an unreasonable volume. Some records reveal everything on the first listen. *Panopticon* is the kind that keeps giving you something new, no matter how many times you return.

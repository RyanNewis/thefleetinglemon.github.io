The Albums That Refuse to Leave

The Fleeting Lemon

Some albums come and go. Others move in, make themselves comfortable, and never quite leave.

They survive changes in taste, questionable hairstyles, new relationships, old friendships and the gradual accumulation of years. Somehow, through all of it, they remain.

It's not necessarily because they're the greatest records ever made. Sometimes they're flawed, uneven or attached to a particular moment in your life. But they have something that keeps drawing you back.

More than just the music

An album can become a time machine. A familiar opening note can take you somewhere you haven't thought about in years. A particular song can bring back a room, a person, a journey or a version of yourself that barely exists anymore.

And sometimes the attachment is harder to explain. There's no grand memory attached. No dramatic story. It's simply a record that still feels right, no matter how many times you've heard it.

The ones that stay

Discovering new music is one of life's great pleasures. But there's something equally satisfying about returning to an old favourite and finding that it still has something to say.

Perhaps that's the real measure of a lasting album. Not how impressive it seemed on the first listen, but whether you still want to hear it years later.

Some records demand your attention. Others quietly earn a permanent place in your life.
